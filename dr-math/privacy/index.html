Dr.Math Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 17, 2026

Dr.Math is a game made by Eaten Frog ("we", "us"). We are responsible for the game under the EU and UK General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and other data protection laws that apply to it. This policy explains what information is used when you play Dr.Math, which is available on Android through Google Play (package name com.eatenfrog.drmath).

In short: Dr.Math has no accounts of its own. Your progress stays on your device, and if you are signed in to Google Play Games it is also backed up to your Google account. If you accept this privacy policy when the game first opens and are 13 or older, your results go to the game's leaderboard once you sign in to Play Games: our leaderboard server receives your Play Games player ID, gamer name and game results, and other players see your gamer name and scores. The game shows ads from Google AdMob, and Google collects some device information to show and measure those ads. The Remove Ads purchase is optional and is handled by Google Play: we never see your payment details. Players under 13 never get personalized ads and never join the leaderboard.

1. Information stored on your device

Your progress (your XP and level, the number of correct answers you gave at tier 5, the highest tier you reached), your settings (sound, vibration, language), your answer to this privacy policy, the birth year you enter on the age screen (section 2), whether you bought Remove Ads, and, if you are signed in, the leaderboard session key and the game results still waiting to be sent (section 5) are saved on your device. They are deleted when you uninstall the game or clear its data, unless they are backed up with Google Play Games (section 4). Android's own device backup, if you have it turned on, may also copy the game's saved data to your Google account; Google handles that backup, and you can turn it off in your phone's settings.

2. Privacy question and age screen

The first time you reach the main menu, the game asks whether you accept this privacy policy, and then asks for your birth year, before any ad is loaded. You can play the whole game whatever you answer. Both answers stay on your phone: the game never sends them anywhere and they are not part of the Play Games cloud save (only Android's device backup, if turned on, copies them with the rest of the game's data). If you clear the game's data or move to a new phone, the game asks again. From the year alone, the game takes the youngest age you could be, so a player whose birthday is still ahead this year gets the younger group's treatment. The game only tells Google which ad treatment to use:

  • Players under 13: ads are treated as directed to children. The advertising ID is not sent, no personalized ads or remarketing are shown, and only ads rated for all ages (G) appear.
  • Players from 13 to 17: Google's teen treatment applies, and no player sees ads rated above PG.
  • Players under 16: Google's consent form is not shown and ads are not personalized.

The answers also decide the leaderboard (section 5): only players who accepted this policy and are 13 or older join it.

3. Advertising (Google AdMob)

Dr.Math shows full-screen ads when you go back to the menu or play again, at most one every two minutes, and an optional reward video that lets you continue a Classic game after it ends. Watching the reward video is always your choice. If you buy Remove Ads (section 6), the full-screen ads stop, and the reward video stays available if you want it. The game has no banner ads. The ads come from Google AdMob. To show ads, measure them and prevent fraud, Google collects:

  • your device's advertising ID and app set ID (the advertising ID is never sent for players under 13),
  • your IP address, from which an approximate location can be estimated,
  • device and app information (model, operating system, language, app version),
  • how you interact with ads, and performance data of the ad software.

In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form is shown after the age screen to players aged 16 and over, and ads are personalized only if you agree. Elsewhere, Google may personalize ads unless you turn this off. You can change your choice at any time under Settings > Privacy options in the game, where it is offered, or in your phone's settings under Google > Ads (or Settings > Privacy > Ads), where you can also reset or delete your advertising ID.

Google acts as an independent company for this data. How Google uses it: policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites. Google's privacy policy: policies.google.com/privacy.

4. Google Play Games

Dr.Math uses Google Play Games Services, which Google provides. Play Games signs you in when the game opens if you use it on your phone, and you can also sign in from the game's settings. When you are signed in, the game uses this Google user data:

  • your Play Games player ID and gamer name, for the game's leaderboard (section 5),
  • your achievements, stored by Google in your Play Games profile,
  • a copy of your progress (XP and level, correct answers at tier 5, the highest tier you reached), saved as a saved game in your Google account's hidden app data space for this game, so you can get it back on a new phone or after reinstalling. The game merges it with the progress on your phone, so progress made on either is kept. The game can only see its own saved game, never your other files.

Apart from the leaderboard described in section 5, this data goes directly between your phone and Google; we do not receive it. The game uses Google user data only to provide these game features. We do not sell it, use it for advertising, transfer it to anyone else, or let any person read it. Signing in is optional, and the game works fully without it. Dr.Math's use of information received from Google Play Games Services follows the Google API Services User Data Policy, including its Limited Use requirements. Google's privacy policy applies: policies.google.com/privacy.

5. The game's leaderboard

The leaderboard ranks players by the total number of correct answers they gave at tier 5 in Classic and Competitive games. It runs on a small server that we operate on Cloudflare. Only players signed in to Google Play Games appear on it, and only if they accepted this privacy policy and entered an age of 13 or older (section 2). A player who did not accept can accept later from the leaderboard screen; from then on new games are sent, never earlier ones. Players under 13 never join, whatever they answered. Until a player may join, and never for players under 13, the game sends nothing to the leaderboard server and does not load the leaderboard. Weekly rewards go only to players on the leaderboard. Practice games are never sent.

If you are signed in and may join, the leaderboard server receives and keeps:

  • your Play Games player ID and gamer name. To make sure nobody can post results in your name, the game asks Google Play Games for a one-time code, and our server exchanges it with Google for your player ID and gamer name. We receive no email address, picture or other Google account data, and we do not keep the code or any Google access token. The server gives you its own random player ID; your Play Games player ID never leaves the server,
  • for each Classic or Competitive game you finish: a random game ID, the mode, the number of correct answers at tier 5, how long you played (without pauses and ads) and when it was sent,
  • your total, the time it last grew and when you first and last used the leaderboard.

To stop abuse, the server counts sign-in requests per IP address for ten minutes at a time; these counters are deleted automatically within about two days. Apart from that, like any web request, reaching the server shows it your IP address and standard technical headers, which the server does not save; Cloudflare keeps short-lived technical logs to run and protect its network and our server. When you open the leaderboard screen without signing in, the game only downloads the list and sends nothing about you.

Other players see your gamer name, your total and your place, never your player ID. We use this data only to run the leaderboard and remove cheating. We do not sell it, use it for advertising, or give it to anyone except Cloudflare, which stores and processes it for us (section 10). If you do not want to appear on the leaderboard, do not accept this policy when the game asks (or do not sign in to Google Play Games for Dr.Math), and ask us to delete what was already sent (section 11).

6. In-app purchase (Google Play)

The game is free to play. You can choose to buy Remove Ads, a one-time purchase that you keep. It is made only through Google Play's billing system, and Google Play's terms apply to it.

  • Payment: Google handles the payment. We never receive your card number, bank details or billing address. How Google handles payment data: Google Payments Privacy Notice.
  • What the game uses: after a purchase, Google Play gives the game the product ID, the purchase's state and a purchase token. The game uses them on your phone to turn off the full-screen ads, confirm the purchase to Google Play and bring the purchase back after a reinstall. It checks purchases with Google Play directly; no server of ours receives them.
  • Saved on your device: only whether you bought Remove Ads. It is not part of the cloud save; after a reinstall the game asks Google Play again.
  • Order records: Google Play shows us records of the orders for our game in its developer console, such as the order number, the product, the price, the date and the country. We use them only for accounting, taxes, refunds and answering your questions.
  • Refunds and purchase history: handled by Google Play. You can see your purchases in the Google Play Store app under your profile icon > Payments & subscriptions > Budget & history.

7. What we do not do

  • Apart from the leaderboard server (section 5), we run no servers. We receive no device, payment or advertising data; apart from the order records in section 6, the data described in sections 3, 4 and 6 is handled by Google.
  • We do not sell personal information for money.
  • We use no analytics or crash reporting services of our own, and Unity's analytics and crash reporting are turned off in the game.
  • The game has no chat and never asks for your name, email address, contacts, location or photos. Other players can see only your gamer name and leaderboard total.

8. Children

Dr.Math is made for players of all ages. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children. The game never asks for a name, email address or any contact information. For players under 13, the game tells Google to treat every ad request as directed to children (section 2). Google then does not use the advertising ID and shows no personalized ads or remarketing. It may still use limited technical information, such as the IP address and device information, only to support internal operations: to show ads based on the game's content, limit how often an ad appears, measure ads and prevent fraud. Only ad software certified for Google Play's Families program is used. Players under 13 never join the leaderboard: even if they accepted this policy, the game sends nothing about them to our leaderboard server. Purchases are made with the payment method of the Google account on the phone; parents can require their approval for every purchase in the Google Play Store app's settings or, for a supervised account, in Google Family Link. For children with a supervised Google account, Google and the parent's Family Link settings decide whether Play Games can be used. A parent who believes their child's information reached our leaderboard can contact us (section 13) and we will delete it.

9. Why this information may be used (legal bases)

  • Personalized ads, and storing or reading ad information on your device where the law requires consent: your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a)). You can withdraw it at any time as described in section 3.
  • Non-personalized ads, ad measurement, frequency limits and fraud prevention: our legitimate interest in funding a free game and keeping ads safe (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).
  • Play Games sign-in, achievements and cloud save: providing the game features you use when you sign in (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)). The leaderboard: your acceptance of this policy when the game asks, together with providing the feature (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) and (b)). Rate limits and removing cheaters: our legitimate interest in a fair leaderboard (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)).
  • The in-app purchase: delivering what you bought (GDPR Art. 6(1)(b)). Keeping order records: our legal duties for accounting and taxes (GDPR Art. 6(1)(c)).
  • Answering your emails: our legitimate interest in replying to you.

10. Where the information goes

Google processes the data described in sections 3, 4 and 6 on servers outside your country, including in the United States. Google describes the safeguards it uses for these transfers, such as the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses, in its privacy policy.

Our leaderboard server runs on Cloudflare, Inc., which processes the leaderboard data for us under its data processing agreement. Cloudflare may process it in the United States and other countries, with safeguards such as the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses.

11. How long information is kept, and how to delete it

  • On your device: until you uninstall the game or clear its data (Android: Settings > Apps > Dr.Math > Storage > Clear storage).
  • Play Games data (cloud save, achievements): until you delete it. In the Google Play Store app, tap your profile icon > Settings > General > Play Games Profile > Privacy & Settings > Your Data > Delete profile & game data, then under "Delete individual game data" find Dr.Math and tap Delete. On the web, go to play.google.com/games/profile > Your Data. You can also delete your whole Play Games profile there. Clear the game's data on your phone first (see above); otherwise the game saves your progress to Play Games again the next time it syncs.
  • Leaderboard data: kept while the leaderboard runs, until you ask us to delete it: email us with your gamer name (section 13) and we delete your player record and game results within 30 days. If you keep playing while signed in, the game creates a new record. If you change your gamer name in Play Games, the leaderboard shows the new one the next time you sign in.
  • Purchase: the purchase data on your phone is deleted with the game's data. Google Play keeps your purchase history in your Google account under its own policy. We keep order records only as long as accounting and tax laws require.
  • Advertising data: kept by Google as described in its privacy policy. You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings, and manage your Google ad data at myadcenter.google.com.
  • Emails you send us: kept only as long as needed to answer you, and deleted within 12 months.

If you cannot delete something yourself, email us (section 13) and we will help within 30 days.

12. Security

All data the game, its Google services and our leaderboard server send over the internet is encrypted in transit (HTTPS/TLS). Data saved on your device is protected by your device's own app sandbox, and we hold no copy of it. The leaderboard server keeps only the data listed in section 5, takes results only with a signed session tied to your Play Games account, and only we can open its database.

13. Your rights and contact

Depending on where you live, you may have the right to access, correct, delete or move your personal data, to object to or restrict its use, and to withdraw consent. We hold no personal data about you except emails you send us, the order record of your purchase (section 6) and, if you signed in, your leaderboard data (section 5). Most other requests are handled by Google through your Google account controls. We will gladly help with any request. You can also complain to your local data protection authority.

U.S. residents: we do not sell personal information for money. Google's personalized ads may count as "selling" or "sharing" under some U.S. state privacy laws. You can opt out under Settings > Privacy options in the game where that option is shown, and at any time by turning off ad personalization in your phone's settings.
